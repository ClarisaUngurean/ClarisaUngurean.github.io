Electromagnetic Waves & Fresnel Equations

A summer reading and research project exploring the mathematics governing electromagnetic waves at material interfaces, with a focus on reflection, transmission, polarization, Fresnel equations, and extensions to lossy media.

INSTITUTION University of Waterloo
YEAR 2026
TOOLS & TOPICS Maple · Electromagnetism · Applied Mathematics · Mathematical Modelling

OVERVIEW

The Project

During the summer, I studied the mathematical description of electromagnetic waves interacting with boundaries between different materials. I worked through analytical derivations, mathematical exercises, and computational exploration of wave behaviour.

The project connected Maxwell's equations with phenomena such as reflection, transmission, polarization, refraction, and the behaviour of electromagnetic waves in both ideal and lossy materials.

MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK

From Maxwell's Equations to Plane Waves

A central part of the project was understanding how the electric and magnetic fields of a plane electromagnetic wave are related. I represented a plane wave using the complex exponential form

$$\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t) = \mathbf{E}_0 e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}-\omega t)}.$$

Substituting this form into Faraday's law,

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t},$$

transforms the spatial and time derivatives into factors involving the wave vector $\mathbf{k}$ and angular frequency $\omega$. This gives

$$\mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{E} = \omega\mathbf{B},$$ $$\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{\omega} \mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{E}.$$

This relationship allowed me to derive the corresponding magnetic field for each incident, reflected, and transmitted wave.

DERIVATION

Describing the Interface

I considered an electromagnetic wave incident on the boundary between two materials. The incident, reflected, and transmitted waves each have their own wave vectors:

$$\mathbf{k}_i,$$ $$\mathbf{k}_r,$$ $$\mathbf{k}_t.$$

Matching the phase of the waves along the interface gives conservation of the component of the wave vector parallel to the boundary:

$$k_{ix} = k_{rx} = k_{tx}.$$

Together with the magnitude relation

$$|\mathbf{k}| = \frac{n\omega}{c},$$

this leads to Snell's law:

$$n_1 \sin\theta_i = n_2 \sin\theta_t.$$

DERIVATION

P-Polarized Waves

I worked through the Fresnel derivation for p-polarized light, where the electric field lies in the plane of incidence.

Because electromagnetic waves are transverse,

$$\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{E}=0.$$

For the transmitted wave, this condition gives a relationship between the $x$ and $z$ components of the electric field:

$$E_{tz} = -\frac{k_{tx}}{k_{tz}}E_{tx}.$$

The magnetic field can then be obtained from

$$\mathbf{H} = \frac{1}{\omega\mu} \mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{E}.$$

Applying these relationships to the incident, reflected, and transmitted waves gives the field components needed to impose the electromagnetic boundary conditions.

BOUNDARY CONDITIONS

Connecting the Waves at the Interface

Maxwell's boundary conditions require the appropriate tangential electric and magnetic field components to match across the interface.

These conditions produce a system of equations relating the incident, reflected, and transmitted field amplitudes. Solving that system leads to the Fresnel reflection and transmission coefficients.

$$r_p = \frac{ n_2\cos\theta_i-n_1\cos\theta_t }{ n_2\cos\theta_i+n_1\cos\theta_t }$$

The coefficients describe how much of the electric-field amplitude is reflected or transmitted when the wave reaches the material boundary.

PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION

What the Equations Predict

POLARIZATION

Brewster's Angle

For p-polarized light, the reflected amplitude can vanish at a particular angle of incidence. This produces Brewster's angle, where the reflected light is completely s-polarized.

REFRACTION

Total Internal Reflection

When light travels from a higher-index material into a lower-index material beyond the critical angle, a propagating transmitted wave is no longer produced.

WAVE PHYSICS

Evanescent Waves

Under total internal reflection, the transmitted field becomes evanescent and decays exponentially away from the material boundary.

ENERGY

Reflection & Transmission

I also explored how the field-amplitude coefficients connect to reflected and transmitted electromagnetic energy.

EXTENSION

Extending the Model to Lossy Media

I extended the analysis beyond ideal dielectric materials to consider lossy media, where electrical conductivity introduces energy dissipation.

This can be represented using a complex material response. The wave number and related optical quantities can therefore become complex, allowing the model to describe both wave propagation and attenuation inside the material.

$$\tilde{k}=k'+ik''.$$

The imaginary component produces exponential attenuation of the wave as it travels through the medium.

COMPUTATIONAL WORK

Exploring the Equations with Maple

Alongside the analytical derivations, I used Maple to work with the mathematical expressions and explore how electromagnetic quantities changed as material properties and angles of incidence were varied.

This helped connect the symbolic Fresnel equations with the physical behaviour they predict.

REFLECTION

What I Took Away From the Project

The project gave me experience working independently through advanced mathematical physics material and following a derivation from fundamental electromagnetic equations to physically measurable behaviour.

It also strengthened my interest in the connection between mathematical modelling, physics, and computation: using equations not only to describe a system, but to understand how changing assumptions or material properties changes its behaviour.
